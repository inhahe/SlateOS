## F-Q10 — [F] Photos and HDR video show with slightly wrong colours. Add colour management, and copy whose? — Status: OPEN (raised 2026-10-05)

**In short:** a picture file can say which "colour space" (the exact shade
each red, green and blue number means) its pixels are in. Most say nothing
or say the ordinary one screens assume (sRGB), and those show correctly
today. But iPhone photos use a wider one (Display P3), some cameras' Adobe
RGB, and HDR video (YouTube's "HDR" videos, many recent films) uses brighter
light in yet another (BT.2020 with a PQ or HLG curve). SlateOS ignores what
the file says and shows every pixel as if it were sRGB, so P3 photos look a
little dull, Adobe RGB ones a little off, and HDR video dim and grey.
Browsers and phones convert each picture to the screen's colours first --
"colour management". Doing that here is a new subsystem: should SlateOS have
it, and whose behaviour should it copy?

**What it involves.** Reading the profile a file carries (an *ICC profile*:
a standard description of a colour space, embedded in PNG, JPEG, WebP, AVIF
and icon files) or the few numbers a video uses instead; converting the
pixels from that space to the screen's (*gamut mapping*: fitting colours one
space has into another); for HDR, mapping light brighter than the screen can
show into what it can (*tone mapping*). Until screens report their own
colours (that needs lane A's display driver to read the monitor's
description), the target is sRGB, which is what nearly every screen
approximates.

**The options.**

| Option | *What changes:* |
|---|---|
| **A.** Copy Chrome: port *skcms*, Skia's colour library (BSD, about 3,000 lines of C), and Chrome's HDR handling | Photos and video show as Chrome shows them, pixel for pixel, as SlateOS already matches Chrome's and libavif's decoding; every result checkable against Chrome. Covers what the web uses (ICC versions 2 and 4, the common kinds of profile). |
| **B.** Copy Little CMS (*lcms2*, MIT; GIMP's, Firefox's optional one) | The most complete library: printer profiles (CMYK), every rendering choice. Colours differ slightly from Chrome's in places. Bigger to port. |
| **C.** Use Firefox's *qcms* (MIT, already Rust) | The least porting -- possibly used as it is. Handles only the common profiles; colours differ slightly from Chrome's; its code would need checking for `unsafe`. |
| **D.** Leave it | Ordinary pictures and video stay right; P3 photos stay dull and HDR video dim. Nothing else breaks. |

**If never answered:** safe -- nothing is blocked and nothing gets worse;
it is option D. The open known issues
`known-issues/F-video-is-shown-without-colour-management.md` and
`F-an-icon-with-a-broken-colour-profile-shows-here-and-not-in-chrome.md`
stay open.

**Claude's recommendation: A.** Every decoder in `gui/imagecodec` and
`gui/video` is held to Chrome's or libavif's pixels, so a Chrome-matching
colour step keeps one standard throughout and keeps every result testable
against a reference. skcms is small, deterministic and well tested, and
Chrome's HDR handling is the one most people see. B is the stronger choice
only if SlateOS is meant to drive printers' colours, which nothing in the
spec asks for.

**Where it bites:** `gui/imagecodec` (each format's profile chunk, read and
dropped today), `gui/video/codec/src/colour.rs` and `gui/video/yuv` (video's
transfer curve and primaries), and the compositor (`gui/compositor`), which
would own the screen's colour space.
