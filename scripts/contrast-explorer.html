Every surface, with the three inks on it

The question the presets above ask you to answer. The desktop draws text on five shaded surfaces, not one — surface0 is the ordinary card (1012 sites), surface1 (542) and surface2 (159) are the deeper ones, and mantle (353) and crust (285) are lighter than the page. A palette naming one card colour has to say what becomes of the other four. Anything below 4.5 is marked.

What the shades are actually used for — and the same thing with borders instead

Taken from the code, not invented. Grepping every use of the three shades in gui/desktop and apps/settings shows they are not three depths of card:

  • surface0 (1012) — the row or card fill: a hovered row, a selected row, a selected day in the calendar, a panel background.
  • surface1 (542) — mostly a border already: the menu border, the popup border, a header separator, badge outlines. Also the fill of a selected row sitting on surface0.
  • surface2 (159) — almost entirely control chrome: the track of a switch in the off position, a close button at rest. Barely a card colour at all.

So the question is narrower than it looked. Nothing in the desktop nests a card three deep; the deepest real stack is page → row → selected row, and one of the three shades is already doing the border job. Ink vs ink — how far apart the four text colours are

pairseparationreads as

The number no contrast checker shows, and the one that decides this question. Every table above asks “can you read this ink on that background”. This asks can you tell two inks apart — which is what makes a caption look like a caption and a link look like a link. Same WCAG ratio, ink against ink instead of ink against paper; 1.00 means the two are one colour to it. Luminance is also the channel that survives colour‑blindness, so when it goes it is the reader this change is for who loses most.
