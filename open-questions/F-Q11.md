## F-Q11 — Video that does not fully say its colours: show it as Chrome does, or as video players do? — OPEN

**In short:** a video stores each pixel as brightness plus two colour
differences, and turning that back into red, green and blue needs a recipe.
Most video names its recipe; some names only part of it, or nothing (older
encodes, many screen and phone recordings, some downloads). For those, Chrome
always uses the old standard-definition recipe, BT.601 -- even for HD video,
which is nearly always made with the HD recipe, BT.709 -- so in Chrome such
a video's reds lean orange and its greens yellow. Video players (mpv, VLC,
Kodi) and SlateOS today use BT.709 for HD and BT.601 for smaller video. You
asked for colour handled as Chrome handles it (F-Q10); this is the one place
found where copying Chrome means copying a choice that is usually wrong.

**What Chrome does** (measured 2026-10-10 in Chrome 154 with test videos,
and in its source). A video's colour has four parts: the *matrix* (the
recipe above), the *range* (whether black and white sit at 0 and 255 or at
16 and 235), the *primaries* (which red, green and blue the numbers mean)
and the *transfer* (the brightness curve). Chrome takes them from the file
around the video only when the file gives all four; otherwise from the video
stream itself, again only if it gives all of them (a VP9 stream's one
colour-space field counts as all four). Where neither does, Chrome uses
BT.601 at studio range and converts nothing. A part given alone is ignored.

| Video | Chrome | SlateOS today |
|---|---|---|
| Says all four | uses them, and converts other primaries and curves to the screen's (being built now, F-Q10) | uses them; no conversion yet |
| Says nothing, HD | BT.601 | BT.709 |
| Says nothing, SD | BT.601 | BT.601 |
| Says some parts only (say, only "BT.709 matrix") | ignores them: BT.601 | uses each part it says, guesses the rest |
| Says only that it is HDR (PQ or HLG) | ignores it: shown as ordinary video, dim and grey | shown as HDR |

**Options.**

- **A. Copy Chrome entirely.**
  *What changes:* HD video that says nothing of its colours shows with
  reds a little orange and greens a little yellow; a video that gives only
  part of its colour is treated as giving none -- an HDR video that says
  only "HDR" shows dim and grey.
  For: SlateOS shows every video exactly as Chrome does; one rule.
  Against: wrong colours for most untagged HD video (Chrome's long-standing
  behaviour, not a deliberate choice anyone defends).
- **B. Copy Chrome wherever the video says all four; keep the players'
  guess where it does not** (recommended).
  *What changes:* nothing for untagged or partly tagged video (today's
  behaviour, which matches mpv, VLC and Kodi); fully tagged video -- what
  the big video sites serve, and standard-definition video that says
  BT.601, BT.2020 video that is not HDR, and Display P3 -- shows exactly as
  in Chrome.
  For: right colours for untagged HD video; Chrome's exact pixels wherever
  the video is fully described.
  Against: untagged and partly tagged video can differ from Chrome; two
  rules where Chrome has one. And "fully described" is Chrome's reading,
  so it carries Chrome's one oddity there: a VP9 HDR video whose stream
  says BT.2020 (which Chrome reads as a whole description) while its file
  says it is HDR but leaves out another part (such as the range) shows dim,
  as in Chrome. Few files are made that way.
- **C. The players' guess, plus every part the video gives converted.**
  *What changes:* a video that gives its primaries but not its matrix gets
  those primaries converted, where Chrome converts nothing.
  For: uses every scrap of information.
  Against: further from Chrome than B, for rare files.

**Recommendation:** B.

**If this is never answered:** SlateOS does B. Nothing is blocked: the
conversions for fully tagged video (F-Q10) are built either way, and only
untagged or partly tagged video would change under A.

**Where it bites:** `gui/video/codec/src/colour.rs` (`resolve`);
design-decisions §1346 (the players' guess, Claude's) and §1379 (HDR).
