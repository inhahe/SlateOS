## C-Q37 — [C] Printing: should SlateOS have its own small print service that talks only to modern "driverless" printers, or a port of CUPS, the print system Linux and macOS use? — Status: OPEN (raised 2026-10-07)

**In short:** nothing in SlateOS can print. You decided in C-Q4 that printing
is a background service programs hand their pages to (`design-decisions.md`
§540), but not *which* service. One choice is a small one written here that
speaks only to printers that need no driver -- nearly every network printer
sold in the last decade. The other is to bring over CUPS, the large existing
system Linux and macOS use, which also drives old printers but is a very
large job. Either way a "Save as PDF" printer comes first, and it is what
lane C builds while this is open.

**Terms, once each:** *driverless* (the printer describes itself and accepts
standard page images, so no per-model driver is needed); *IPP Everywhere* and
*AirPrint* (the two standards for that, which nearly every printer since about
2013 supports); *CUPS* (the print system of Linux and macOS: a spooler, i.e. a
queue that holds jobs, plus converters and per-model drivers).

**Options.**

| | What prints | *What changes:* |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Our own service, driverless only** | network printers that speak IPP Everywhere or AirPrint, and Save as PDF | modern printers print with no setup; a printer that needs a driver (an old USB inkjet, say) cannot print |
| **B. Port CUPS** | the printers Linux supports, old ones too once their drivers are ported | the same printer list as Linux, after a port many times the size of A |
| **C. A now, and CUPS's drivers later behind it** for printers that need one | A's printers now; old ones when someone ports their drivers | as A today; old printers later, without programs changing how they print |

- **A**, for: small, and built on SlateOS's own pieces -- the job format that
  exists (`gui/printjob`), the service registry, capabilities for who may
  print -- where CUPS brings its own users-and-groups permissions. It is also
  where CUPS itself is heading: CUPS 2.3 deprecated per-model drivers in
  favour of driverless printing. Against: printers without IPP Everywhere or
  AirPrint -- mostly older and cheap USB ones -- do not print.
- **B**, for: decades of printer support. Against: CUPS needs its converters
  (and through them Ghostscript or Poppler, each a large port of its own) and a
  network-discovery daemon; and programs would talk CUPS's protocol rather than
  the job format §540 chose, so the service you decided on would become
  CUPS's.
- **C**, for: A's simplicity now with a path to old printers that does not
  disturb programs. Against: a second route to maintain once drivers arrive.

**If never answered:** nothing prints today either way; Save as PDF is built
regardless, since every option needs it. Only real printers wait on this.

**Claude's recommendation:** **C.** It is A until somebody needs an old
printer, and A covers the printers people actually buy. Meanwhile lane C
builds what every option needs: the pages a program sends, drawn in the
toolkit's own drawing commands, and Save as PDF.

**Also worth knowing:** text in a saved PDF that can be searched and copied
needs each letter's outline from the font engine, which lane F has been asked
for (`requests/c-f-an-outline-for-each-shaped-glyph.md`); printers that take
page images do not need it.

**Where it bites:** `gui/printjob` (the job format, which gains the pages);
the service, `gui/printd` (new); the desktop's orphaned
`gui/desktop/src/print_manager.rs`, whose queue and dialog models move into
the service; `roadmap-detailed.md` §2 *Printing*.
