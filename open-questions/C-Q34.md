## C-Q34 — [C] On a display scaled to 150% or 200%, programs still draw at 100%. Should the compositor enlarge what they draw, or should every program enlarge itself? — Status: OPEN (raised 2026-10-05)

**In short:** on a high-resolution screen you set the scale to 150% or 200%
so that things are not tiny. Today the desktop's taskbar, menus and window
frames grow, but every program's contents stay at 100% -- text, buttons,
pictures all as small as without the setting
(`known-issues/TD-C-PROGRAMS-DRAW-AT-ONE-SCALE-WHATEVER-THE-DISPLAYS-IS.md`).
Programs here do not hand over pictures of themselves; they hand the
compositor (the part that puts every window on the screen) a list of shapes
and text to draw. So the enlarging can happen in one of two places, and
the choice decides which parts of the system change.

**Two terms.** *Logical pixels*: the sizes a program writes, the same on
every screen. *Physical pixels*: the screen's own dots -- at 200%, two
across for every logical one.

| Option | *What changes* |
|---|---|
| **A. The compositor draws each program's list at the display's scale** (recommended) | every program grows at once, untouched; a program lays out in logical pixels and the compositor multiplies what it draws, and divides where the pointer is before telling the program; a window moved to a display of another scale redraws at that scale by itself |
| **B. Every program enlarges itself** | the toolkit learns the scale as it has just learned the text size, and each program multiplies its own layout; nothing changes in the compositor; programs grow one by one, as each is changed |

**For A:** one change, in one place (lane F's compositor), and every program
-- including ones ported from elsewhere and ones nobody maintains -- is
right; text and shapes stay sharp, because they are drawn at the screen's
resolution from the list, not stretched as a picture would be. **Against
A:** a picture a program draws pixel by pixel (a game's frame, a photo
viewer's image) is enlarged too, so a program that wants every physical
pixel must say so; and the compositor's drawing and hit testing take a
factor they do not have now.

**For B:** a program sees physical pixels and decides; nothing new in the
compositor. **Against B:** two hundred programs to change, each a chance to
get it wrong (a click landing beside what was drawn), and every new one must
remember.

**Under either** the text size the user chooses (§1474) stays separate: it
enlarges text and what is laid out round it, not pictures, and applies on
any display.

**If it is never answered:** nothing breaks -- on a display at 100%, which
is every display unless someone sets otherwise, nothing differs. On a
scaled one, programs stay small, as now.
